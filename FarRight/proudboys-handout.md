# Primary Source Handout: Proud Boys — Selected Texts (2017–2019)

**For classroom use — selected passages with editorial framing**

*The Proud Boys were founded in 2016 by media personality Gavin McInnes. Within two years the organization grew from an internet in-joke to a national network with hundreds of chapters. Members played prominent roles in the 2020 election disputes and the January 6, 2021 assault on the U.S. Capitol, after which the organization was designated a terrorist entity in Canada and several members were convicted of seditious conspiracy in the United States. These passages are drawn from the Proud Boys' official magazine website (proudboysusa.com), archived via the Wayback Machine. They represent the organization's own self-presentation at an early stage — not a description by critics or law enforcement.*

*A note on the texts: Three of the four passages below appeared in a single 48-hour period in late August 2017, just days after the Charlottesville "Unite the Right" rally, at which one person was killed. The fourth appeared in 2019, as the Proud Boys were under increased scrutiny. The timing is analytically significant.*

## Passage 1: The Organization's Self-Description

> "The Proud Boys are a men's organization founded in 2016 by Vice Media co-founder Gavin McInnes. McInnes has described the Proud Boys as a pro-Western fraternal organization for men who refuse to apologize for creating the modern world; aka Western Chauvinists."
>
> The official tenets:
>
> "Minimal Government / Maximum Freedom / Anti-Political Correctness / Anti-Drug War / Closed Borders / Anti-Racial Guilt / Anti-Racism / Pro-Free Speech (1st Amendment) / Pro-Gun Rights (2nd Amendment) / Glorifying the Entrepreneur / Venerating the Housewife / Reinstating a Spirit of Western Chauvinism"
>
> "Though these are our central tenets, all that is required to become a Proud Boy is that a man declare he is 'a Western chauvinist who refuses to apologize for creating the modern world.' We do not discriminate based upon race or sexual orientation/preference. We are not an 'ism', 'ist', or 'phobic' that fits the Left's narrative. We truly believe that the West Is The Best and welcome those who believe in the same tenets as us."

*— "Proud Boys: Who Are They?" Proud Boy Magazine, August 24, 2017*

**[Discussion prompt: The tenets list is heterogeneous — it mixes constitutional positions (1st and 2nd Amendment) with social positions ("Venerating the Housewife"), economic positions ("Glorifying the Entrepreneur"), and the identity claim ("Reinstating a Spirit of Western Chauvinism"). What holds these together? Compare the Proud Boys' ideological bundle to the JBS tenets from Dallek Ch. 1 — what overlaps, and what does the Proud Boys add or subtract? What does the simultaneous claim of "Anti-Racism" and "Closed Borders" tell you about how the organization manages an internal tension?]**

## Passage 2: Distancing from the Alt-Right

Written by Gavin McInnes four days after Charlottesville, this is the organization's official policy on white nationalism:

> "The two big differences we have with them [the Alt-Right] is the 'JQ' and racial identity politics. They think the Jews are responsible for America's problems and they think 'Western' is inseparable from 'white.' They don't see a future for non-whites in America. FUCK THAT. We reject all of those things. We openly encourage Jewish and non-white members and want them to know they're at home with us..."
>
> "To be clear: All white nationalists / anti-Semites are banned from Proud Boys even if they never bring up said topics."
>
> "We wish the Alt-Right AND the Alt-Left would go fight it out amongst themselves and leave us alone. As Henry Kissinger said when he was asked who would win the Iran / Iraq war, 'Ugh, can't they both lose?'"
>
> "I've said this 1,000 times and I'll say it 1,000 more: We are a fraternal organization like the Elk's Lodge. 100% of us are Western chauvinists which simply means we all believe the West is the Best."

*— Gavin McInnes, "WE ARE NOT ALT-RIGHT," Proud Boy Magazine, August 21, 2017*

**[Discussion prompt: McInnes is drawing a sharp line between "Western chauvinist" and "white nationalist" — insisting that "Western" is not the same as "white." How persuasive is this distinction? What work does it do organizationally? Van Dyke and Soule's militia movement data (Week 3) showed that the Patriot/militia movement coded as ideologically white supremacist even while sometimes formally rejecting explicit racism. Does this pattern look familiar? Also note the Elk's Lodge comparison: McVeigh et al. described the 1960s KKK as an organization that functioned through pre-existing social networks. The Proud Boys are claiming to be in that social tradition — a drinking club, a fraternal order. What does that framing accomplish?]**

## Passage 3: The Confederacy in the Room — Charlottesville and Monument Destruction

"Based Odin" is identified as "a second degree Proud Boy in the United Kingdom." This passage appeared the same day as Passage 1, three days after Charlottesville:

> "It's no secret that the far-left are mentally deranged zombies... Now we have new evidence to suggest the further degradation of the left wing mentality, something altogether insidious and sly: the rising trend of destroying historical monuments. You've seen the chaos in Charlottesville and read the steady stream of headlines suggesting which historical statues should be torn asunder next. These Bolshevik Bastards don't want these monuments destroyed because of some crusade to right the wrongs of the past. They want to delete these marble memoirs from the pages of history so they can rewrite it to fit their hysterical agenda. Now that is real Hitler tier bullshit. One of the trademarks of pure fascism."
>
> [On Antifa:] "If this were to come to fruition, what would the endgame be? If you think about it, whole organisations like Antifa could be transformed into docile slaves ready to serve the sane and proud world. This could not be considered inhumane as they already live as slaves; they just serve the wrong masters. The modern slave is not bound by race or religion, they would be achieving everything they ever wanted, a multi-ethnic society of dribbling spastics working together to make the world more prosperous for us proud westerners."

*— "Based Odin" (UK member), "Lobotomise the Left," Proud Boy Magazine, August 24, 2017*

**[Discussion prompt: This is a UK Proud Boy writing about Charlottesville — pointing to the organization's transnational reach from very early on. Set aside the violent fantasy for a moment and focus on the monument framing: removing Confederate monuments is "real Hitler tier bullshit" and "a trademark of pure fascism." This is an extraordinary rhetorical inversion — who is the fascist in this passage? Compare this move to Byman's point (Week 3) about Reconstruction-era framings where KKK violence was presented as defensive. Who is the aggressor and who is the defender in "Based Odin"'s account? Also: this author calls for a "multi-ethnic society" of slaves — how does that coexist with the official claim that Proud Boys are anti-racist?]**

## Passage 4: Defining "Western Chauvinist"

Written in March 2019 by a contributor named Lloyd Somerset:

> "A Western Chauvinist is a proponent of Western Civilization, someone who supports a secular government whose legal code is informed by Judeo-Christian ethics and whose origins lie in the Greco-Roman tradition of the Republic."
>
> "When Gavin McInnes describes the tenets of the Proud Boys, he clearly defines Western Chauvinist as such: 'it just means a nationalist, a patriot,' i.e. 'the West is the best.'"
>
> On immigration and membership:
>
> "While there are Proud Boys in Western countries across the globe, many of us feel that there is one country that exemplifies the ideal manifestation of Western aims: The United States of America. One of the founding principles of the U.S.A. is the phrase E PLURIBUS UNUM... This concept, perhaps unique to the U.S., means that not only are foreigners welcome to come here, but they are also encouraged (and expected) to become like us and adopt our ways."
>
> "Becoming an American is contingent upon the following: 1) Become a U.S. citizen, via legal channels. 2) Swear an oath of allegiance to the United States. 3) Respect and Revere our laws."

*— Lloyd Somerset, "What is a Western Chauvinist?" Proud Boy Magazine, March 20, 2019*

**[Discussion prompt: "E PLURIBUS UNUM" — from many, one — is being deployed here as an assimilationist argument: you can become part of the West, but only by becoming like "us." Compare this to the BNP's "we accept that Britain always will have ethnic minorities... as long as they remain minorities and do not change... the fundamental culture." Are these the same argument in different national registers? Both claim openness while asserting the priority of the dominant culture. Now ask: is "Western civilization" a racial category, a cultural category, a civilizational category, or some combination? What difference does the answer make to the claim that Proud Boys are "not racist"? Finally: how does this 2019 piece's more polished, definitional tone differ from the August 2017 material — and why might the organization want a more careful self-presentation two years in?]**

## Reading Notes for Instructors

**On the "fringe vs. seed" question (connecting to Session 3B):** The Proud Boys are explicitly named in Dallek Chapter 12 as part of the January 6 coalition — heirs to the Bircher tradition of combining fraternal organizing with conspiratorial politics and anti-government grievance. But the Proud Boys are not an anti-communist organization, and they don't map cleanly onto the JBS template. They represent a new synthesis: fraternal/social-movement hybrid, internet-native, ideologically eclectic. The question of whether they are a continuation or a mutation of the older tradition is worth pressing.

**On the diversity of the US far right:** Put the Proud Boys next to the three Week 3 cases. The KKK used terrorist violence for electoral suppression (Byman). The 1960s KKK restructured social networks around racial resentment (McVeigh et al.). The militia/Patriot movement mobilized on economic restructuring and anti-New World Order ideology (Van Dyke & Soule). The Proud Boys are a 21st-century men's fraternal organization that uses street brawling as recruitment theater, claims to be multiracial, and produced January 6 seditious conspiracy convictions. These are not the same thing. Yet they share something: each constructs an in-group under threat from a corrupt establishment and a racialized (or civilizational) out-group.

**On the Civil War link:** The monument passage (Passage 3) is the sharpest connection. Charlottesville's "Unite the Right" rally was explicitly about defending a Robert E. Lee statue; the Proud Boys declined to attend but wrote a defense of the monuments within 72 hours. The Reconstruction parallels are instructive: Byman shows that the original KKK used violence to "protect Southern heritage" against federal encroachment. The Proud Boys are defending the same monuments a century and a half later, calling their removal "fascist." The frame has inverted, but the object of defense is the same.

**On US/European comparisons:** "Based Odin" is a UK member — the Proud Boys had international chapters from early on. Yet the ideological content is distinctly American: the 1st and 2nd Amendments, E PLURIBUS UNUM, the specific Charlottesville monument dispute. The BNP frames its exclusion in ethnic terms ("indigenous peoples of the British Isles"). The Proud Boys frame theirs in civilizational and behavioral terms ("adopt our ways"). Which framing is better equipped to travel internationally? Which is more susceptible to racial reinterpretation from within? The 2021 seditious conspiracy convictions suggest that the "fraternal drinking club" self-presentation did not determine the organization's ultimate trajectory.

**On the Finchelstein question:** Are the Proud Boys fascist, populist, or something else? They participate in elections (Trump support is near-universal per McInnes) but their most notable action was attacking the Capitol to prevent an electoral outcome. They have no fascist program for government. They do claim to represent authentic American values against a corrupt globalist elite. Finchelstein's populism frame captures much of this — but the violence and the anti-democratic Capitol action push back toward the fascist end of his spectrum.
